**Day 53: A Summer Across the Pacific**

英语词汇小故事 · 第53天（70词）

**第一部分　英文故事**

Last summer my **dad** and I left **Shanghai**, flew across **the Pacific Ocean** to **Tokyo**, and then went on to **Ottawa**. The sun **arose** over the sea, and a quiet hope had **arisen** in my heart that this would be the finest trip of my life. On the plane I **drew** a map of our route, and I had already **drawn** the same map twice in my notebook before we landed.

In that city we watched the **Olympic Games**. The big **boat race** **began** at **seven** o\'clock, and when we reached the river it had already **begun**. **Eight** teams **chose** their boats, and every crew had **chosen** a lane. A cold wind **blew** across the water, and the flags had **blown** straight all morning. A tall **Swiss** rower **caught** his oar and pulled hard, while an **Arab** and a **Moslem** friend cheered from the bank. A **New Zealander** in a green shirt **drank** some water, and I had already **drunk** mine.

We **drove** to a quiet **phone booth** to call home, though I had never **driven** a car before. Nearby, a kind **woman** sold fruit, and a young **Miss.** helped a **Tibetan** visitor find the **Lost** office. We **took** the number **seventy** bus, and after we had **taken** our seats, an old man **bit** into an apple that a dog had already **bitten**. The wind **threw** dust into the air, and the rubbish had been **thrown** into a bin by a cleaner.

**How** and **where** we would go next, no one could say. **Many** people said the weather would be **worse** than at home, but **most** of them had never seen the **worst** of a storm. We had **less** time and **much** to carry, yet **neither** of us complained, and **either** road was fine. By the lake stood an old temple the people called **sacred**, and beside it a wall that had **bent** in the long **war** years ago. An old soldier told us he had been **beaten** in battle but was never **stricken** by fear.

Later we met **Mr.** and **Mrs.** Chen, a couple from **Hong Kong** traveling with **the British** team. They said they had **eaten** fish for breakfast, but I **ate** only bread because I was **tired**. The town clock **struck** **ten** as we packed, and we still needed **more** time, though we had the **least** trouble of the whole trip. That afternoon we flew to **Tibet** and looked far toward **the Antarctic**. In **nine** days we would fly home, and in **twelve** months we would tell this story again.

**第二部分　中文译文**

去年夏天，我和爸爸离开上海，飞越太平洋到达东京，接着又去了渥太华。太阳从海面升起，我心里也升起一个安静的希望，觉得这会是人生中最美好的一次旅行。在飞机上，我画了一张我们路线的地图，而在降落之前，我早已在笔记本里把同样的地图画了两遍。

在那座城市里，我们观看了奥运会。盛大的划船比赛七点钟开始，等我们赶到河边时，比赛已经开始了。八支队伍选择了各自的赛艇，每支队伍都选定了自己的赛道。一阵冷风吹过水面，旗帜整个上午都被吹得笔直。一位高大的瑞士划手抓住船桨奋力划动，而一位阿拉伯人和一位伊斯兰教徒朋友在岸边欢呼。一位穿绿衬衫的新西兰人喝了些水，我也早已喝完了自己的。

我们开车来到一个安静的公用电话间打电话回家，尽管我以前从没开过车。附近，一位和善的妇女在卖水果，一位年轻的小姐帮一位西藏游客找到了失物招领处。我们坐上了七十路公交车，坐下之后，一位老人咬了一口苹果，而那苹果已经被一条狗咬过了。风把尘土抛向空中，垃圾也早已被清洁工扔进了垃圾桶。

我们接下来怎么走、去哪里，谁也说不准。许多人说那里的天气会比家乡更糟，但他们大多数从未见过最猛烈的暴风雨。我们的时间更少了，要带的东西却很多，可是我们俩谁也没有抱怨，走哪条路都行。湖边矗立着一座人们称之为神圣的古老寺庙，旁边是一堵在许多年前的战争中弯折了的墙。一位老兵告诉我们，他在战斗中被击败过，却从未被恐惧击倒。

后来我们遇见了陈先生和陈太太，一对来自香港、随英国队一起旅行的夫妇。他们说早餐吃了鱼，而我因为太累只吃了面包。镇上的钟敲了十下，我们打包时还需要更多时间，不过这一路我们遇到的麻烦最少。那天下午我们飞往西藏，远远望向南极。再过九天我们就要飞回家，而十二个月之后，我们会把这个故事再讲一遍。
